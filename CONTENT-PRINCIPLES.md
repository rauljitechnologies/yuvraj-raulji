# Content Principles — AI-Driven, Not AI-Generated

**Source of truth for all copy, articles and optimization work on yuvrajraulji.com.**
Read this before writing or editing any content, including section copy, blog articles,
metadata and SEO/AEO changes.

---

## The core distinction

**AI-DRIVEN ≠ AI-GENERATED.**

AI is a research, analysis, optimization and decision-support layer. It never replaces
experience, judgment, opinion or voice. Published content must read as though written by an
experienced e-commerce technology consultant — because it was.

> AI helps me think better, research faster, analyze more data, automate work and spot
> opportunities. I provide the experience, judgment, strategy, opinion and final decision.

**Use AI for:** research, topic discovery, search-intent analysis, keyword clustering,
competitor and content-gap analysis, technical and data analysis, content structure, question
discovery, SERP and entity analysis, internal-link recommendations, SEO/AEO optimization,
quality checking, conversion/UX/performance analysis, trend detection, automation.

**The published result must be:** human-led, expert-led, first-hand, original, specific,
evidence-based, opinionated where appropriate, contextual, useful, natural.

---

## Hard rules

### 1. Never invent experience

No fabricated case studies, metrics, clients, project outcomes or technical implementations.
**If information is unavailable, mark it as needing confirmation rather than filling the gap.**
This will legitimately block work — that is intended.

### 2. Never publish generic AI filler

Banned: "In today's rapidly evolving digital landscape…", "Businesses need to embrace
innovative solutions…", "AI is revolutionizing…", "Unlock the power of…", "Revolutionize
your…", "Transform your business…", "Seamlessly…", "Cutting-edge…", "Game-changing…",
"Next-generation…" (unless literally accurate).

Every substantive passage should carry **WHAT + WHY + HOW + WHEN + TRADE-OFFS + REAL
EXPERIENCE + REAL EXAMPLES**.

### 3. Write from experience, not from keywords

Start with *"What useful knowledge can I provide?"* — never *"What keyword should we rank
for?"* SEO organizes expertise; it does not manufacture it.

### 4. Show technical judgment

Never recommend every technology for every business. Explain trade-offs and say when something
is the wrong choice.

> **Weak:** "Headless commerce provides unparalleled flexibility."
>
> **Right:** "Headless commerce separates the commerce engine from the customer-facing
> experience. That can make sense when a business needs independent frontend releases,
> multiple customer experiences or a more flexible architecture — but it also introduces
> additional infrastructure and operational complexity. For a smaller store with
> straightforward requirements, a well-built traditional Shopify or Magento implementation may
> be the better engineering decision."

### 5. First person, used with restraint

"I've worked across…", "I generally recommend…", "One issue I frequently see…", "I would not
recommend…", "The trade-off is…". Balance personal experience with educational content.

## The quality test

1. **Could another AI generate almost the same article?** ← if yes, it is not ready
2. Does it contain my own expertise?
3. Specific reasoning?
4. Practical examples?
5. Trade-offs explained?
6. More than a search summary?
7. Does it sound like me?
8. Are all claims accurate?
9. Is anything invented?
10. Would an experienced professional find it useful?

Every important article should contain at least one of: real experience · a real example · a
technical decision · a trade-off · a framework · a checklist · a practical recommendation · an
original perspective · a real implementation lesson.

## Workflow

Identify the problem → analyze search intent → analyze keywords and entities → analyze the
existing results → identify gaps → research the technical background → build an expert outline
→ **add real experience and point of view** → draft → humanize and edit → strip generic AI
language → verify claims → add examples and trade-offs → optimize headings and internal links
→ optimize SEO/AEO → optimize conversion → final human-expertise review.

---

## Topics, SEO and freshness

- **Answer real questions.** "When Should You Upgrade Magento 2 Instead of Replatforming?"
  beats "Complete Guide to Magento 2".
- **Original frameworks** are welcome where they reflect real reasoning — never as keyword
  vehicles.
- **AI topics** must be practical: what changes technically and operationally, what data is
  required, what the risks are, what to automate, what stays human-controlled, the ROI, the
  architecture, the trade-offs. No generic "AI trends" pieces.
- **No forced keywords.** Primary topic → semantic entities → supporting concepts → related
  questions → internal links → useful content.
- **AEO:** question → direct answer → explanation → example → trade-off → related topic. No
  artificial FAQ padding.
- **Freshness:** update when technology genuinely changes. **Never bump publication dates just
  to look fresh.** Never publish placeholder articles with backdated publish dates.
- **Sources:** verify against official primary documentation. Understand and restate in your
  own words — never copy.
